No need for a thick book that looks professional and feels like it never ends. So the book ends here. It already says quite a lot.

Learning to program, you will run into plenty of trouble. But it's worth it: programming is talking to machines, and one day, to robots.

You believe there will be lots of robots in the future too, right? Maybe there will even be a war between robots and humans, like in \emph{The Matrix}. In a world like that, you'll need to truly understand how systems work. You'll have to talk to the robots in programming languages, in assembly code, in 0s and 1s---and maybe even fight them with voltage glitches, current surges and electromagnetic pulses.

So you think programming in C is hard right now? Think again.

\bigskip

OK, I know you've made it to this page and may still feel you can't do a thing. You're staring at the iPad in your hands, wondering: where do I start? Which app do I download? This book doesn't match the thing in my hands at all!

Your first challenge: set up a programming environment. Type in Hello, world, compile it, run it. Stuck? Flip back through this book, again and again. A word you don't know? Ask DeepSeek!
